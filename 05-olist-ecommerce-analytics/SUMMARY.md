# Executive summary: Olist marketplace

**Decision: invest in on-time delivery of the first order, starting with Rio de Janeiro, rather than in retention campaigns.**

## Why

- **Growth comes from new customers.** GMV grew 140% year on year (Jan–Aug 2018 vs 2017). Only 3.0% of customers ever buy a second time, and returning customers place 3.3% of orders.
- **A late delivery costs about two review stars:** 1.97 stars lower after controlling for category, state, month, order value and freight (95% CI 1.93–2.01). Reviews are what new customers see.
- **The problem is concentrated.** Rio de Janeiro, the second-largest market, is late on 12.1% of orders against 4.5% in São Paulo, despite a median seller distance of only 394 km. Matching São Paulo would have avoided about 940 late orders.
- **Retention has a low ceiling.** Repeat buying is weakly predictable (the best model's top 10% of customers return at 1.8× the average) and not measurably reduced by a late first delivery (odds ratio 0.90, 95% CI 0.74–1.10).

## Actions

1. Audit carrier and last-mile performance in Rio de Janeiro.
2. Book extra carrier capacity before Black Friday and the post-holiday period, when the late rate at least doubled.
3. Track the late rate by state as a weekly KPI; set regional delivery promises for the north-east.
4. Use the repeat-propensity model only for low-cost targeting, such as which first-time buyers receive a follow-up offer.

## Confidence

- **High** that late deliveries are associated with much worse reviews: large, precise, stable under controls.
- **Medium** on cause and effect: observational data. A test of delivery promises in one region would confirm it.
- Data ends August 2018.
