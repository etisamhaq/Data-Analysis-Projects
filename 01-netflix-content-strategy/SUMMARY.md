# Executive summary: Netflix content strategy

**Decision (for a competing streamer): compete on series that retain subscribers, commit to regional markets for years rather than launch spikes, move early on family content, and launch with a content-based recommender.**

## Why

- **Netflix is shifting back to TV.** TV's share of new additions rose from 25% (2018) to 34% (2021), with the odds rising 15% a year (p = 2.5×10⁻⁸), while total additions levelled off after 2019. Series keep subscribers watching; title count matters less.
- **Regional bets reverse.** India's share of country mentions rose from 2.4% (2016) to 18.2% (2018), then fell to 7.1% (2021). Both moves are far beyond chance.
- **Family content is coming back:** from 10.1% of additions (2018) to 16.7% (2021) (p = 6.6×10⁻¹¹).
- **More back catalogue:** the share of movies added within a year of release fell from 75% (2016) to 40% (2021).
- **A recommender works with no viewing data.** Recommendations built from descriptions alone match genre 21.8 points more often than random titles of the same type (95% CI 20.5–23.0), so it can serve new users and new titles from day one.

## Actions

1. Benchmark against Netflix's TV hours, not its catalogue size.
2. Plan regional expansion as a multi-year commitment with milestones.
3. Prioritise family and kids content now, while Netflix is still rebuilding it.
4. Ship a content-based recommender at launch; blend in viewing-based methods once data exists.

## Confidence

- **High** on the direction of the trends (large samples, significant tests).
- **Medium** on their size: the data is a 2021 snapshot, so titles that left Netflix are missing and early years are under-counted.
- These are counts of titles added, not viewing hours.
